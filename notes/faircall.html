Notes · Project 05

FairCall

Python · ML · team project, in progress · 3 min read

The question

Umpires miss around 10 ball-strike calls a game. Are those misses random, or do they lean in predictable ways, like on two-strike counts or at the edges of the zone?

Why it's useful

MLB is moving toward letting teams challenge ball-strike calls. Knowing when and where umpires tend to miss tells a team when a challenge is worth it, and makes the game fairer.

What I did

  • Team project with Akhil Munipalli and Soham Panigrahi for Applied Machine Learning at Stevens.
  • Used MLB Statcast data for every taken pitch: location, count, batter and pitch type.
  • Trained models to predict whether each pitch is called a strike, on 2024 and tested on 2025.
  • Rated every umpire's accuracy, with a 95% range on every number.

What I found

In 2025 umpires matched the measured strike zone 92.9% of the time, about 10.8 missed calls a game. On pitches within 2 inches of the edge, that drops to 73%.

The best model predicts the umpire's call 94% of the time, across 368,845 pitches.

With the new challenge system in 2026, challenges fixed about a quarter of umpire misses. Bigger misses were more likely to be challenged, but most misses of every size still stood.

What surprised me

How much umpires struggle at the edges. They get about 93% of calls right overall, but only 73% of pitches within 2 inches of the edge of the zone.

What I'd do next

Add plain-language report cards for each umpire to the site, and keep the challenge data updated each season.
